# Content strategy, topic clusters and content planning

## Topic clusters
- A cluster = one pillar page covering a broad topic + supporting pages covering specific subtopics in depth, all interlinked (supporting → pillar with descriptive anchors; pillar → each supporting page).
- Clusters build topical authority and give search engines a clear site structure. Each page in the cluster targets a distinct intent/keyword set to avoid cannibalization.
- Build clusters from real demand: group Search Console queries and keyword research by shared meaning/intent; pick the most comprehensive URL as pillar.

## Content planning
- Prioritize by business value and opportunity: (1) improve pages already ranking 4–20, (2) fill gaps where competitors rank and you have no page, (3) new clusters that support money pages.
- Plan a mix: refreshes and consolidations often outperform new posts for established sites.
- Schedule realistically; consistency beats volume. Avoid publishing many near-duplicate pages targeting tiny keyword variations.

## Writing briefs
- A good brief contains: target query and intent, audience and their problem, the angle/unique value, outline (H2/H3) derived from user questions and SERP analysis, entities and facts to include, internal links (to/from), evidence needed (data, photos, expert input), CTA, title/description drafts, word-count range based on intent (not a fixed number).

## Content refresh signals
- Declining clicks/impressions over 28–90 days, outdated years/prices, competitors with fresher or deeper content, queries the page ranks for but doesn't answer.

## Content gap analysis
- Compare competitor topics/URLs/titles with your coverage; prioritize gaps that match your audience and business, and that you can cover with genuine expertise. Don't copy competitor content — create a better, original resource.
