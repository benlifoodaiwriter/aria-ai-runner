# Semantic SEO, entities and topical coverage

## Why semantics matter
- Modern search understands meaning, synonyms and relationships between entities (people, products, places, brands, concepts). Pages rank for many related queries, not only the exact keyword.
- A page that covers the subtopics, attributes and questions people associate with a topic satisfies more intents and earns broader visibility.

## How to cover a topic semantically
- Identify the main entity and its attributes (e.g., for "espresso machine": pressure (bar), boiler type, grinder, milk frother, capacity, power, cleaning, price range, brands).
- Cover related entities and comparisons (espresso vs. moka pot, single vs. dual boiler).
- Answer the questions people ask (People Also Ask, forum questions, support tickets, GSC queries).
- Use natural variations: synonyms, plurals, morphological variants, transliterations. Do not force exact-match repetition.
- Define terms the first time they are used; keep terminology consistent.

## Entity clarity
- Name the entity explicitly and unambiguously early in the page (brand + model, full product name).
- Use structured data (Product, Organization, Person, Article) with stable identifiers (sku, gtin, mpn, sameAs) to disambiguate.
- Link to authoritative pages about the entity where useful for readers.

## Measuring coverage
- Compare your page's terms and subtopics with what top-ranking pages and real user questions include; fill genuine gaps with useful information, never by stuffing lists of terms.
- Track the number of distinct queries a page earns impressions for in Search Console: growing query breadth indicates better semantic coverage.
