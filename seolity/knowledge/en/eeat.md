# E-E-A-T — experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust

## The concept
- E-E-A-T comes from Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines. It is not a single ranking factor, but Google's systems look for signals that align with it. Trust is the most important element; the others support it.
- YMYL ("Your Money or Your Life") topics — health, finance, safety, legal, civic information — require the highest level of E-E-A-T.

## Signals a page can show
- Experience: first-hand evidence — original photos, measurements, test results, "we used it for 3 months" details, screenshots of real use, before/after.
- Expertise: author with relevant credentials or demonstrated skill; accurate terminology; correct, complete explanations.
- Authoritativeness: the site/author is a recognized source on the topic (mentions, citations, links from reputable sites, consistent topical focus).
- Trust: accurate content, clear ownership, contact details, return/refund and shipping policies (for shops), secure checkout (HTTPS), transparent reviews, editorial and correction policy.

## Practical recommendations
- Show a byline linking to an author page with bio, credentials and other articles; use Person structured data with sameAs links where real profiles exist.
- Add "About us", contact information, physical address (for local businesses), and business registration/trust badges that are real and verifiable.
- Cite primary sources for claims, statistics and medical/financial statements.
- Show "last reviewed by" for YMYL content only when a real qualified reviewer did it.
- For product reviews: demonstrate hands-on use, compare alternatives, explain how you tested, show pros and cons; avoid affiliate-only thin summaries.

## What an AI must never do
- Invent authors, credentials, reviews, awards, certifications, testimonials or "studies show" claims.
- Add trust badges or ratings that do not exist.
- Present AI-generated opinions as first-hand experience.
