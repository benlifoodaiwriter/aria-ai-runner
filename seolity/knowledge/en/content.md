# Helpful, people-first content and on-page optimization

## What search engines reward
- Google's ranking systems aim to surface content created primarily to help people, not to manipulate rankings. Since March 2024 the "helpful content" signals are part of the core ranking systems.
- Ask of every page: Would someone who arrived directly (not from search) find it useful? Does it show first-hand experience or real expertise? Does a reader leave satisfied, or do they need to search again?
- How content is produced (human, AI-assisted) is not the criterion; usefulness and originality are. Mass-producing pages mainly to rank is "scaled content abuse" under Google's spam policies regardless of the method.

## Rewriting rules for an AI optimizer
- Preserve facts, figures, prices, specifications, names, links, shortcodes, embeds and the author's voice. Never invent statistics, studies, awards, guarantees or quotes.
- Improve clarity first: answer the main question early (inverted pyramid), then add depth.
- Add what is missing for the search intent (steps, comparisons, pros/cons, specs, examples, FAQs), not filler.
- Use the focus keyword naturally in the title/H1, the first paragraph and at least one subheading when it reads naturally. Use variants and related terms instead of repeating the exact phrase.
- Keyword density is not a ranking factor. Repetition that hurts readability is a negative signal (keyword stuffing is a spam policy violation).
- Never hide text (same color as background, off-screen, font-size 0) and never show crawlers different content from users (cloaking).

## Structure and readability
- One H1 per page that matches the page topic; H2/H3 hierarchy that mirrors the reader's questions. Do not skip levels for styling.
- Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences), descriptive subheadings, lists and tables for scannable facts.
- Prefer concrete language: numbers with units, named entities, specific steps.
- Long is not automatically better. Match depth to intent: a definition query needs a crisp answer; a buying guide needs comparisons and criteria.

## Thin, duplicate and outdated content
- Thin: pages with little unique value (boilerplate, auto-generated text, near-empty category pages). Improve, merge into a stronger page (301), or noindex if the page must exist for users.
- Duplicate/near-duplicate: consolidate to one canonical URL; use rel=canonical for unavoidable variants.
- Outdated: refresh facts, prices, years and screenshots; update the visible "last updated" date only when the content materially changed.

## On-page checklist
- Unique, descriptive title and meta description.
- Clean, readable URL slug (short, hyphenated, no stop-word clutter).
- Descriptive internal links to and from related pages.
- Images with descriptive alt text, dimensions, compressed modern formats.
- Structured data that matches visible content.
- Fast, stable, mobile-friendly rendering (Core Web Vitals: LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile).
