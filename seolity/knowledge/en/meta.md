# Title tags, meta descriptions and CTR optimization

## Title tags (title links)
- Every indexable page needs a unique, descriptive <title>. Google may rewrite titles that are missing, duplicated, boilerplate, keyword-stuffed or misleading; it uses H1, anchor text and other on-page text instead.
- There is no official character limit, but desktop results truncate around 600 px (≈50–60 Latin characters; Persian glyphs have different widths, so measure pixels, not characters).
- Put the most important words first: primary topic/keyword, then the distinguishing benefit, then the brand (separated by "|" or "–"). Brand-first only for brand/homepage queries.
- Match the page's intent: "Buy …" for transactional pages, "How to …" / "Guide" for informational pages, "X vs Y" for comparisons.
- Avoid: ALL CAPS, repeated keywords, clickbait that the page does not deliver, dates that are not maintained.

## Meta descriptions
- Not a ranking factor, but often used as the snippet and therefore influences CTR. Google rewrites descriptions when a passage better matches the query.
- Aim for ≈ 120–160 characters (≈ 920 px on desktop). Summarize the page's specific value; include the main keyword naturally (it is bolded when it matches the query).
- Include concrete differentiators: price range, specifications, free shipping, warranty, number of steps, last update — only when true.
- Unique per page; avoid quotes that cut the snippet and avoid boilerplate across categories.

## CTR optimization with Search Console data
- Compare each page's CTR with the expected CTR for its average position. Pages with many impressions, a top-10 position and CTR clearly below expected are the best candidates for title/description rewrites.
- Typical expected organic CTR (varies by SERP features and intent): position 1 ≈ 25–35%, 2 ≈ 12–18%, 3 ≈ 8–11%, 4–5 ≈ 5–7%, 6–10 ≈ 2–4%.
- Rewrite for the dominant queries of the page (from GSC), not for a guessed keyword.
- Treat every rewrite as an experiment: record the date, wait at least 2–4 weeks, compare equal windows and account for seasonality and position changes.

## Rules for AI-generated titles/descriptions
- Never promise what the page doesn't contain (discounts, "free", "best price") or invent numbers.
- Keep the language of the page; keep brand names and model numbers exact.
- Provide several options with different angles (benefit-led, question-led, specificity-led) so a human can choose.
